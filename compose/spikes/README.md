# Verify-first spikes

Throwaway checks of the facts listed under "0. Verify first" in
[`../docs/roadmap.md`](../docs/roadmap.md). Each one settles an assumption the spec makes about
Raven or about a dependency, before implementation starts. Results are dated 2026-11-06, run on
macOS (arm64) against Raven 0.2.1 at `8b1e7d4`, unless a row gives its own date.

| Spike | How to run | What it checks |
|---|---|---|
| [`raven/`](raven/) | `raven/run.sh` | Raven's own behaviour, headless (null backend, null platform) |
| [`deps/`](deps/) | `cd deps && gradle -q resolve` | that phase 3 dependencies resolve for `macosArm64`, `linuxX64` and `mingwX64` without Compose UI |
| [`nested-frames/`](nested-frames/) | `./gradlew :spikes:nested-frames:allTests`, from `compose/` | Compose inside the nested frames of Raven's blocking dialogs, headless, through `RavenHost` |
| [`macos-child-views/`](macos-child-views/) | `macos-child-views/run.sh`, on macOS with MoltenVK | GL and Metal in Raven's native child views, in a real window, read back and captured on screen |
| [`thorvg/`](thorvg/) | `thorvg/run.sh` (a real window, macOS) and `./gradlew :spikes:thorvg:allTests`, after `brew install thorvg` | ThorVG's C API rendering a Lottie animation over a custom-drawn widget, and from Kotlin/Native |
| [`navigation3/`](navigation3/) | `./gradlew :spikes:navigation3:allTests`, from `compose/` | Navigation 3's back stack and saveable-state decorator under the host, with the serialization plugin |
| [`maps/`](maps/) | `maps/launch-maps` (a real window, macOS; online, or `MAPS_OFFLINE=1` after `maps/fetch-data`; drawn by Raven's Map widget through compose-raven-map) and `./gradlew :spikes:maps:allTests` | OpenStreetMap vector tiles, styled by MapLibre's style JSON and fetched as the map needs them, drawn by Raven's Map widget. First drawn by compose-raven itself on a `Canvas` (the result below); that drawing moved into Raven's C and was retired from the spike ([ADR 0002](../docs/adr/0002-rendering-in-c-bindings-thin.md)) |

| `Rxml_document_parse` registers no `<Widget>` declarations | **Passes** | `raven/spikes.c`: `Rxml_is_widget_registered` is false after parsing, and the `<Widget>` stays an unexpanded node | `parseRavenXml` needs no clean-up |

No fork change |

| Compose recomposes safely inside the nested frames of Raven's blocking dialogs (checked 2026-11-11, Raven 0.2.3 at `c2f072d`) | **Passes** | `nested-frames/`: `Rmessage_box`, `Rdialog_run` and `Rfile_dialog_open`, each called from a coroutine on `RavenDispatcher`. While one waits, state writes recompose and apply in its frames and Text follows, a controlled Checkbox in another window reverts a refused click and keeps an accepted one, and every apply has exactly one `applyChanges` on the stack, never one inside another. Work queued behind the blocked coroutine and writes from another thread are composed within a millisecond, with animations on or off. Removing the dialog's window from the composition, or `Rquit`, ends the call with Cancel and leaks nothing | `native = true` stays in phase 3, with the [caveats below](#nested-frames-caveats) |

Every backend reports committed text only | `TextField` writes back as soon as the app answers, with no deferral. Check again if Raven gains preedit (`NSTextInputClient`, `text-input-v3`) |

| OpenStreetMap vector tiles can be drawn by compose-raven itself, without a native map library (checked 2026-11-14, at `cff8993`, with seven Raven additions) | **Passes**, once Raven draws meshes and capped polylines in one call, meshes without anti-aliasing, turned text, and parts of a texture, turned too | `maps/`: OpenFreeMap's Mapbox Vector Tiles of central Chicago (zooms 12 to 14) are decoded in Kotlin (protobuf by hand), their polygons triangulated by a port of earcut, and styled by OpenFreeMap's Liberty style JSON, read and interpreted in part: fill, fill-extrusion (drawn flat, opaque) and line layers, their filters, and zoom-interpolated colours, opacities and widths. They draw on a `Canvas`, pan by dragging and zoom with the wheel, with tiles prepared on worker threads. Captures at zooms 12.5, 14, 15.5 and 16.5 look like Liberty without its labels. **Drawn one call a primitive**, with `Rdraw_triangle` and `Rdraw_line`, the cost was in the calls: headless, the same view (43,903 triangles, 36,140 segments) took 12.7 to 13.2 ms of fills and 13.0 to 13.4 ms of lines, medians of 30 frames; and Raven anti-aliases each triangle's edges, so neighbours showed the background along the edges they share. **With the additions**, `Rdraw_triangles` (a mesh of one colour in one call, with offset and scale, filled without anti-aliasing: the new `R_INSTANCE_ALIASED` instance flag, which the shader fills by edge functions, a pixel on a shared edge going to exactly one triangle) and `Rdraw_polyline` (many lines in one call, a NaN point between them), the same view takes 0.3 ms of fills and 2.2 ms of lines: fills about 40 times faster, lines 6. In the window, the Loop at zoom 14 (110,212 triangles, 75,614 segments, 12 tiles) draws in about 8 ms, rendered in one GPU draw in under 1 ms, with no seam, translucent fills included. Raven's `test_render` checks both (`test_meshes`: an opaque square's diagonal at full colour, a translucent one's at the inside's colour all along, which fails on all 60 pixels if a shared edge is drawn twice; offset and scale; lines broken at NaN). Preparing a dense tile (530 KB) takes about 95 ms to decode and 210 ms to style and triangulate, on a worker thread | A map module drawn in Kotlin is feasible. **Since superseded**: the map moved into Raven's C as the Map widget ([0140](../docs/issues/archive/0140-a-native-map-widget.md)), 6 to 14 times faster at preparing tiles, and the spike's Kotlin drawing was retired ([0142](../docs/issues/archive/0142-the-map-spikes-kotlin-drawing.md), [ADR 0002](../docs/adr/0002-rendering-in-c-bindings-thin.md)); it stays in git history, on `spike/maps`. `Rdraw_triangles`, `Rdraw_polyline`, `R_INSTANCE_ALIASED`, `Rdraw_text_rotated`, `Rdraw_image_region`, `Rdraw_image_rotated` and `Rdraw_polyline`'s caps are fork changes to propose, with `DrawScope.drawTriangles`, `drawPolyline(cap)` (and a `StrokeCap`), `drawText(rotation, fontWeight, fontStyle)` and `drawImage(image, srcOffset, srcSize, ..., rotation)` over them. **A mesh's outer edges are aliased too**, so each fill draws its outline over them, one device pixel wide, as MapLibre's `fill-antialias` does; the frame stays under 8 ms. **Point labels** (places, water names, points of interest, airports) are placed as Liberty's symbol layers say in part: `text-field` expressions, `text-transform`, `text-size`, wrapping at `text-max-width`, `text-anchor`, `text-offset`, colour and halo, and collision on a grid, the layers higher in the style first. At zoom 14 they place 142 labels of 1,361 in about 4 ms, the frame in 13 ms: each line of text is drawn nine times, eight for its halo. **Labels along lines** (street and river names) need rotated text, which Raven didn't draw (a glyph was an axis-aligned quad): `Rdraw_text_rotated` adds it, a fourth fork change, under `DrawScope.drawText(rotation)`. The glyphs are laid out unturned, then the vertex shader turns each quad about the text's corner (`R_INSTANCE_ROTATED`, the angle and the point in the glyph's unused radii); `test_rotated_text` checks a quarter turn lands where it should and nowhere else. A label is placed every `symbol-spacing` along its line, glyph by glyph at the line's angle where each stands, reading left to right, and left out where the line turns more than 45 degrees between two glyphs. Placing every frame, 149 labels of 1,874 at zoom 14 cost 10.1 ms, the frame 18 ms. **Placement is now kept** and moves with the map, as MapLibre's labels do between placements: through a pan of up to a quarter of the view or a zoom of up to a quarter of a level, the text keeping its size, and placed again past those or 200 ms after the camera stops. The halo is four diagonal copies instead of eight. Headless (unoptimised test build, medians of 30 frames), labels cost 2.0 to 2.4 ms a frame panning or zooming, 29 or 30 of 30 frames reusing the placement, against 16.3 to 17.3 ms placing every frame; in the window, the zoom 14 frame takes 6.2 ms, labels 0.7 of it, and a placement 4.2 ms when it runs. **Icons**: Liberty's sprite sheet (its `@2x` JSON index and PNG) draws points of interest, transit, airports, city dots and road shields, icon by icon, with `Rdraw_image_region`, a fifth fork change, under `DrawScope.drawImage(image, srcOffset, srcSize, topLeft, size)`; `test_image_region` checks a quarter of a texture fills its square. A symbol's icon is centred on its point, its text placed around it by the style's anchor and offset; both must fit. Road shields, placed by a zoom expression (points, then lines), are upright symbols every `symbol-spacing` along their roads, the number on the icon. **One-way arrows** turn to their roads where they stand, plus the layer's `icon-rotate` (180 degrees for a road one way against its line), with `Rdraw_image_rotated`, a sixth fork change, under `drawImage(rotation)`: drawn as a turned glyph's quad, a texture times the tint, so the vertex shader that turns rotated text turns it, with no shader change; `test_image_rotated` checks a quarter turn. An arrow is never flipped to read left to right: its direction is the point. Mixing the sprite sheet's texture with the glyph atlas's takes the GPU draws from 1 to about 45 a frame. **Fonts**: each layer's `text-font` chooses a face, regular, bold or italic, through `DrawScope.drawText(fontWeight, fontStyle)`: Raven's default font, with the faces it derives (Liberty's Noto Sans itself is left out); labels are measured in the face they are drawn in. **The deepest tiles** (zoom 14) draw every zoom past theirs, so they are prepared with every layer drawn from them on: points of interest and minor street names start at zoom 15. **Also left out of labels**: letter spacing, halo blur and fading. Each triangle is still its own instance, a quad the fragment shader fills: a real vertex mesh would cut the paint further, but isn't needed at these sizes. **Line styling**: each layer's `line-cap` ends its lines round, cut (butt, the default) or square, with `Rdraw_polyline`'s new cap, a seventh fork change: the line primitive's two ends can be cut square in the shader (`R_INSTANCE_BUTT_START`, `R_INSTANCE_BUTT_END`), while a polyline's turns stay round; `test_line_caps` checks each cap past both ends and a cut line's round turn. `line-dasharray` cuts lines into dashes in line widths, the pattern starting again on each line, and `line-gap-width` draws a line twice, either side of a gap (an offset with mitred corners, at most four times the offset at sharp ones). Both are cut on screen for the width drawn, kept per tile and layer, and cut again when that width changes by more than a hundredth. Paths and park outlines come out dashed, state lines dotted, and rail lines hatched with their ties, short cut dashes that round caps would make dots. **Left out of the drawing**: line joins other than round (a cut line's turns are round), `line-offset`, patterns, and data-driven paint. **Live tiles**: online by default, the spike fetches Liberty's style and its source's TileJSON, then each tile as the map needs it, with Ktor 3.6.1 (Darwin engine on macOS, curl on Linux), at most 8 requests at a time, into a disk cache (`~/Library/Caches/compose-raven-maps-spike`, by the planet build's tile URL). Any zoom from 0 to the source's 14 is drawn, deeper zooms from the zoom-14 tiles; while a tile loads, the nearest ancestor ready stands in. Each frame tells the store the tiles it wants: the others stop loading; past 160 prepared tiles, the least recently used go; a tile that failed waits 10 s before it is asked for again. Chicago at zoom 14 (30 tiles fetched) is whole about 2 s after the window opens; Paris at zoom 13.5 fetches 21 tiles, and the next launch takes all 22 from the cache; zoom 4 over the United States draws from the low zooms' tiles, labels included, in 6.5 ms a frame. `TileStoreTest` checks the store against a source of hand-made tiles (loaded once, cancelled when no longer wanted, evicted least recently used, retried after a failure) and the cache. Not checked: panning by hand over the network. **Data**: OpenFreeMap requires its attribution, shown on the map; `MAPS_OFFLINE` draws `maps/fetch-data`'s tiles of central Chicago, kept out of git |

### Nested frames: caveats

- **Calls that wait together return in reverse order.** A second blocking call runs inside the
  first one's frames, so closing the first dialog doesn't resume its coroutine until the second
  has closed too (`stackedDialogsReturnInReverseOrder`).
- **No longer applies (2026-11-11): the first nested frame came at once only because a dialog needs
  a layout.** While the blocked block ran, `RavenDispatcher` still counted its drain task as
  posted, so a dispatch queued behind it neither posted a task nor woke Raven, and only the layout
  of the dialog's content made Raven frame without sleeping. Each drain now arms a guard timer due
  at once, so the nested loop wakes with or without a layout:
  [RavenDispatcher wakes for work queued behind a blocked block](../docs/issues/archive/0059-ravendispatcher-wakes-for-work-behind-a-blocked-block.md).
- **Most "native" dialogs are Raven's.** `Rmessage_box` is always Raven's own sheet. `Rfile_dialog_*`
  uses the OS only on Windows (`IFileDialog`, inside Windows' own modal loop, not checked) and on
  Linux with zenity or kdialog (waited for with nested frames every 30 ms). On macOS, `native = true`
  shows the same Raven dialogs as the default, only blocking.
